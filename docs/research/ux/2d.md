## Where the engine starts

- **The 2D renderer** (`engine_render2d`, ADR 0015): a `Canvas` of 64-byte quads, sprites
  with hard pixel edges and antialiased SDF rectangles, circles, capsule lines and
  outlines; every quad carries its bindless texture, so a scene of sprites from any number
  of images is one batch per blend mode and clip rectangle; order by layer, then a key
  (`Order::y_sorted` for y), then submission order, by a radix sort that allocates nothing;
  `Camera2d` (y up) and `View2d::pixels`; glyph quads for text. On ref-1, 100,000 8-pixel
  sprites take 164 µs on the RTX and 2.5 ms on the Radeon.
- **Viewports** (ADR 0016): nesting, render ticks, pixel-perfect integer scaling; the frame
  renderer (ADR 0072) renders at a fixed `render_size` and scales it by integers, nearest.
- **2D physics** (`engine_physics2d`, ADR 0028, ADR 0066): Box2D v3.1.1 behind a C shim:
  static, kinematic and dynamic bodies with boxes, circles and capsules on 64 query layers,
  ray and shape casts, overlaps, begin-contact events, a capsule mover.
- **What a scene has:** nothing 2D. The player draws `engine_render2d` canvases only for the
  game's UI (`Game::ui`, screen, world or camera placed); `engine_physics2d` is used by no
  other crate. `Camera` is perspective only. There are no tile maps, tile sets, slices,
  flipbooks or 2D lights.
- **The editor** has the tools 2D will reuse: the 3D viewport with picking from an id
  buffer and outlines (ADR 0109), the gizmo and keyboard transforms (Q, E, R with typed
  values), the owner's snapping rules (free by default, Shift for steps, Ctrl to the grid,
  G toggles), F to frame and Ctrl+1..9 bookmarks, drops from the dock on the surface under
  the pointer, Tab's editing of brushes' faces and characters' joints, the colliders'
  overlay (Alt+C), engine-drawn previews (ADR 0112), import settings in the inspector (E4-9,
  `texture.compression = Uncompressed` for pixel art), tabs for graphs (ADR 0123) and the
  Animation tab (Alt+N, ADR 0124) at the bottom, and the `Animator` with clips, events and
  graphs.

## Tile painting

- **What works:**
  - the left button paints and the right one erases (Godot, LDtk), so erasing needs no
    tool; Shift draws a line, Ctrl+Shift a rectangle (Godot); a held key for the picker
    (Godot's Ctrl+click, Unity's Ctrl);
  - a block picked on the sheet or captured from the map is a stamp (Tiled's right drag,
    Godot's patterns, Unity's Brush Picks);
  - turning and flipping while placing (Godot's Z, X, C, V; Unity's brackets; Tiled's X, Y,
    Z);
  - random from a selection, with a chance to place nothing (Godot's scattering), weighted
    by the tiles' probabilities (Tiled);
  - the other layers dimmed while one is painted (Godot);
  - fill limited to touching cells or to every cell of the same tile (Godot's Contiguous,
    Unity's).
- **What annoys:** three key maps for the same tools; an eraser that is a tool to pick
  (Unity's D, Tiled's E); painting into the wrong layer when its name is all that shows.
- **What we take:**
  - **Tab over the viewport on a tile map paints**, as it edits brushes' faces and
    characters' joints; Tab or Escape leaves. A header in the viewport says the layer and
    the tool, so the mode is never hidden.
  - left paints, right erases; Shift a line, Ctrl+Shift a rectangle, Ctrl+click picks (a
    drag picks a block: a stamp); B fills touching cells, Shift+B every cell of that tile;
    Z and X turn, C and V flip (Godot's keys, as the team works from Godot's layout);
    PageUp and PageDown change the layer, the others dimmed. The tools are modifiers, not
    keys to switch, so P keeps playing in place and the transform keys stay what they are;
    the panel's bar shows them for the mouse;
  - **the Tiles panel** at the bottom (Alt+T): the layer's tile set at any zoom, its
    patterns (blocks saved in the tile set, Godot's) and its terrains; a tile or block
    clicked there is the brush;
  - random: several tiles picked paint at random by their weights, chosen by a hash of the
    cell, so painting a cell again gives the same tile; scattering (a chance of nothing);
  - **one undo step a stroke**, shown in the frame of the edit (4 bytes a cell, a chunk
    uploaded once a frame); no stroke stalls the UI;
  - the camera's bookmarks keep Ctrl+1..9; stamps are kept as patterns, not on number keys.

## Auto-tiling rules

- **What works:** painting what a cell is and letting the art follow (LDtk's IntGrid,
  Unity's Rule Tiles, Tiled's terrain brush); a standard layout made into rules in one go
  (LDtk's Rules Assistant; GameMaker's 16- and 47-tile templates*); the missing
  combinations shown (Tiled's Patterns); a rule's effect seen while it is edited (LDtk).
- **What annoys:** bits painted on 47 tiles one by one (Godot, Tiled) or rules clicked one
  by one (Unity); a solver that picks tiles by the stroke's order (Godot); results that
  change when the stroke runs the other way.
- **What we take:**
  - the tile set's tab has **Terrains**: + New Terrain (a name and a colour), its mode
    **Blob** (walls and platforms against anything else) or **Corners** (ground where
    several terrains meet; tiles drawn half a cell off the painted grid, the dual grid);
  - **Apply Layout**: a drag over the sheet where a standard layout sits (the 47-tile blob,
    the 16-tile blob with turns, the 16-tile corner set, Godot 3's 3 × 3 minimal) fills
    every bit in one command; bits can still be painted tile by tile afterwards;
  - the panel lists **missing combinations**, with how many cells of the open scenes need
    each (a click shows them), and what is drawn there meanwhile (the nearest tile);
  - a terrain layer is painted with terrains (the Tiles panel's Terrains), and its tiles
    are made in the frame of the edit, the same every time (questions 4 and 5);
  - **decoration rules** after terrains (LDtk's model): a rule's pattern edited as LDtk does
    (left requires, right forbids, middle clears, a drag paints), its tiles picked on the
    sheet, chance, flips, modulo; the cells a hovered rule changes are outlined in the view.

## Sprite slicing and atlases

- **What works:** slicing by a grid of size or count (Godot, Unity, Aseprite); islands of
  opaque pixels (Unity's Automatic, Paper2D's double-click); re-slicing that keeps names
  (Unity's Smart and Safe); pivot presets (Unity); nine-slice borders and pivots kept in
  the art file (Aseprite's slices); Aseprite files used as they are (Unity's importer).
- **What annoys:** an asset per sprite (Paper2D; Unity's Tile assets); custom data lost on
  re-import (Unity's importer without UUIDs); atlases configured for batching (Unity's
  Sprite Atlases), which ours does not need (ADR 0015).
- **What we take:**
  - a double-click on an image in the dock opens **its own tab** (as graphs and code do):
    the image at whole-number zooms with a pixel grid, its slices drawn over it;
  - **Slice**: Grid (cell size or count, margin, gap), Islands (opaque regions, with a
    minimum size), or by hand (drag a rectangle; a double-click inside fits it to the
    connected pixels, Paper2D's way); names made (`run_0`, `run_1`, …) and renamable;
  - slicing again keeps the names and ids of the slices whose rectangles stay (Unity's
    Smart), so scenes that use them do not break;
  - pivots by nine presets or dragged, snapped to pixels; nine-slice borders by dragging
    four lines; a slice's collision outline traced from its pixels (below);
  - slices are parts of the image (`hero.png#run_3`, ADR 0108), listed under it in the dock
    and in asset fields;
  - **Aseprite files** (`.aseprite`, `.ase`) need no slicing: their frames, tags and slices
    are read as they are, and a change saved in Aseprite shows at once (E4-5's reload);
  - no atlas settings: images are bindless (ADR 0015); the cook packs only where measured
    to pay.

## Flipbooks

- **What works:** frames picked on the sheet with the mouse (Godot's dialog); tags and
  per-frame durations made in the art tool (Aseprite); a preview on hover (Paper2D); one
  clip kind for frames and other fields (Godot's AnimationPlayer, Unity's clips); events
  on frames (Unity's importer from cel user data).
- **What annoys:** two flipbook systems (Godot); one speed for all frames (Godot's
  SpriteFrames, Paper2D's frame counts); frames ordered by name (Paper2D); a controller
  asset made behind the developer's back (Unity), read-only (Unity's importer).
- **What we take:**
  - in the image's tab, frames picked (click, Shift+click a range, Ctrl+click), then
    **Make Clip** (M): a name, the frames with their durations (100 ms each by default, an
    Aseprite file's own), loop, once or ping-pong;
  - the image's clips listed in its inspector with the engine's preview, as a model's
    clips are (ADR 0124's Clips: ▶, scrubbing, Auto Play, P plays in place);
  - **a sprite's clip plays through the `Animator`** (question 6), so graphs, cross-fades
    (a cut for frames), events and saves come with it; in the Animation tab its frames are
    a row of Step keys, and E adds an event on a frame (a footstep on frame 3);
  - an Aseprite tag is a clip; a cel's user data `event:<name>` is an event (Unity's
    importer's convention).

## The 2D view and its navigation

- **What works:** the wheel zooms about the pointer; the middle button or Space with a
  drag pans (Godot, Aseprite*, Tiled*); 1:1 and fit; a pixel grid when close (Aseprite*);
  rulers and guides (Godot); snapping to pixels and to the grid (Godot); the same view for
  sprites and UI (Godot).
- **What annoys:** two workspaces to switch between (Godot); a 2D switch that only flattens
  a 3D view (Unity*); zoom factors that are not whole numbers, at which pixel art shimmers
  in the editor.
- **What we take:**
  - **one viewport:** it turns 2D when the scene's main camera is orthographic, or by its 2D
    switch (the palette's View: 2D); 3D content is not drawn in it in E13 (ADR 0125);
  - orthographic onto XY, y up; the middle button with a drag pans (Space is already the
    gizmo's local axes, so it stays that); the wheel zooms about the pointer, **in
    whole-number steps of the art's pixels** (100 %, 200 %, 300 %, …; below 100 %,
    halves), so pixel art never shimmers in the editor; Ctrl+0 is 1:1; F frames the
    selection and Ctrl+1..9 keep bookmarks, as in 3D;
  - a grid of pixels from 800 %, the selected tile map's cells, and the game camera's frame
    at its reference size;
  - snapping under the owner's rules: free by default, Shift steps by one pixel of the art,
    Ctrl snaps to the tile grid, G toggles;
  - the gizmo in 2D (x and y arrows, a square for both, a ring turning about z, scale
    handles); keyboard transforms as in 3D (Q X 16 Enter);
  - sprites picked by their visible pixels (the id buffer, ADR 0109) and outlined; Alt+click
    lists what is under the pointer (E2-3c);
  - an image dropped from the dock is a sprite at the pointer, its pivot on the snapped
    point; a tile set dropped on a tile map's row in the tree gives it that tile set.

## 2D physics shapes

- **What works:** shapes drawn on the tile in the tile set (Godot, Tiled, Paper2D); one
  shape painted onto many tiles (Godot's property painting); a full rectangle by one key
  (Godot's F); outlines traced from a sprite's pixels, simplified, with a preview (Godot's
  Sprite2D menu); tiles merged into outlines (Unity's Composite).
- **What annoys:** seams between tiles that catch characters (Godot before 4.5, Unity
  without a composite); the composite added by hand (Unity); outlines with no inside
  (Unity).
- **What we take:**
  - in the tile set's tab, **Shapes**: tiles selected (a box), then F (full) or a preset
    (half, slope 45°, slope 22.5° low and high), applied to all of them; or drawn: Tab on a
    tile adds points on edges, drags them on the pixel grid, Delete removes them; one way
    per tile; friction and a surface name per tile (for footsteps);
  - nothing to add for seams: a layer collides as merged outlines by default
    (`TileMap.collides`), seen with Alt+C;
  - a sprite's `Collider2d`: box, circle, capsule or polygon; **Trace from Pixels** (alpha
    threshold, simplification) with a preview; Tab on the entity edits its points in the
    view, as faces are edited;
  - occluders for 2D lights the same way (Trace from Pixels, or per tile in the tile set).

## What we take

1. **One world and one viewport** (question 1): 2D entities are entities in the XY plane;
   the viewport turns 2D, with the same picking, gizmo, keys, bookmarks, undo and inspector.
2. **Painting as Tab's mode** on a tile map: right erases, Shift line, Ctrl+Shift
   rectangle, Ctrl+click picks, B fills, Godot's Z, X, C, V; the Tiles panel (Alt+T).
3. **Terrains by layout templates first,** bits by hand second, missing combinations
   listed; rules after (question 5).
4. **Slicing in the image's tab,** re-slicing that keeps names; slices as parts of the
   image; Aseprite files read as they are (question 7).
5. **Flipbooks as clips of the `Animator`** (question 6), made from frames picked on the
   sheet or from Aseprite tags, events on frames.
6. **Whole-number zooms of the art** in the 2D view.
7. **Collision without a step to add:** tile shapes by presets and property-style
   application, merged outlines by default, sprite outlines traced from pixels.
8. **As everywhere:** every edit one undo step in the one history; tile sets and images'
   settings autosaved and written by Ctrl+S; edits in Play apply at once (E3); the panels
   allocate nothing per frame and stay within ADR 0107's 1 ms of UI.

## What we do not take

- **Two workspaces** (Godot): the 2D view is the viewport's.
- **An asset per tile, sprite or flipbook** (Unity's Tile assets, Paper2D): tiles live in
  the tile set, slices in the image's `.meta`, clips in the image's `.meta` or the
  Aseprite file.
- **Atlases to configure** (Unity's Sprite Atlases): nothing to batch (ADR 0015).
- **A terrain solver over painted tiles** (Godot): terrains are stored, tiles made from
  them (question 4).
- **An eraser on Shift** (Unity): Shift draws lines, and the right button erases.
- **Stamps on the number keys** (Tiled): the bookmarks have them.
- **A level editor apart from the engine** (LDtk, Tiled): their best ideas inside ours;
  importing their files is a later step (E13-7).

### E13's steps

| Step | What | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| **E13-1. Sprites in scenes** | `Sprite`; the orthographic camera with a reference size; the player's 2D pass (layers, y-sort, sort groups); the 2D view (pan, whole-number zooms, grids, snapping, picking by pixels, the 2D gizmo, outlines); images dropped as sprites | everything later is drawn and edited through it |
| **E13-2. Slicing and flipbooks** | the image's tab (grid, islands, by hand, re-slice, pivots, nine-slice); slices as parts; Aseprite files; clips made from frames, played by the `Animator`, events on frames | sprites need their frames before tiles need their sheets |
| **E13-3. Tile maps** | `.tileset` and its tab (tiles, shapes, animation, weights, data); `TileMap` with its cells in the scene; painting in Tab's mode and the Tiles panel | the largest tool; stands on E13-1's view |
| **E13-4. Terrains and rules** | terrain sets (blob, corners), layout templates, missing combinations, terrain layers made as they are painted; then decoration rules | stands on E13-3's tile sets and painting |
| **E13-5. 2D physics** | bodies and colliders in scenes; shapes in the tile set's tab; Trace from Pixels; merged outlines seen with Alt+C | the shapes are drawn in E13-3's tab |
| **E13-6. 2D lights** | lights and occluders; their ranges drawn as sound ranges are; occluders traced or per tile | needs sprites and tile sets |
| **E13-7. Later** | sub-pixel smooth cameras, isometric and hexagonal grids, LDtk and Tiled imports, sprite shapes along splines, 2D bones | each improves the first six, and none blocks them |

Measured as they land: painting 1,000 cells in one stroke within the frame; the Tiles panel,
the tile set's tab and the image's tab within ADR 0107's 1 ms of UI; no allocation with the
panels open in Play.

## Questions for the team

The UX questions are ADR 0125's: 1 (2D in the one world), 3 (where a map's cells are kept),
4 and 5 (terrain layers, terrains and rules), 6 (flipbooks), 7 (where sprite settings live),
8 (grid shapes) and 10 (scripts' calls). Each there has its context, options with costs and
a recommendation.
