# 2D: sprites in scenes, and drawing by hand

A 2D game is made of the same entities as a 3D one: a sprite is an entity with a `Sprite`
component, lying in the XY plane, y up, turned about z. An orthographic camera looks at
them; the player draws them every frame with `engine_render2d`, sorted the way you say, and
moves them smoothly between the simulation's ticks. The design and its measurements are in
ADR 0125 (its E13-1a section).

Sections 1–7 are about sprites in scenes; section 8 onwards about drawing quads by hand into
a canvas (UI, debug drawing, effects of your own).

## 1. A first 2D scene

```rust
use engine_app::prelude::*;
use engine_app::Sprite;
use engine_runtime::Camera;

// A pixel-art camera: 320 × 180 pixels of art, 16 pixels a metre, shown at the largest
// whole multiple of 320 × 180 that fits the window.
world.spawn((Transform::IDENTITY, Camera::pixel_art([320, 180], 16.0)));

// A sprite at (2, 1.5) metres, from an image the scene names (its path and id, as a
// scene file holds them).
world.spawn((
    Transform::from_xyz(2.0, 1.5, 0.0),
    GlobalTransform::IDENTITY,
    Sprite::new(AssetRef::file(hero_image)).on_layer(1, 0),
));
```

In a scene file (what the editor writes) the same sprite reads:

```text
[entity 6c1f0a3e9d2b47c88e51f0d4a7b3c912]
Name = "Hero"
Sprite.image = asset("art/hero.png", 5f2c0a3e9d2b47c88e51f0d4a7b3c912)
Sprite.layer = 1
Transform.translation = (2, 1.5, 0)
```

- An entity spawned in code needs its `GlobalTransform` (a scene file's get theirs when
  loaded); a sprite is drawn once a tick has placed it.
- The images load in the background, as a material's do; the first frame after a scene is
  loaded waits for them, so the scene shows whole.
- Only an orthographic main camera draws sprites. A perspective camera draws the 3D world
  and none of the 2D one; a 3D game pays nothing for 2D (no pass, nothing recorded), and a
  2D view draws none of the 3D scene (mixing them waits for an ADR of its own).

## 2. Units: metres, and pixels a metre

Positions and sizes are metres, in 2D as in 3D (physics, sound ranges and navigation all
agree). An image says how many of its pixels make a metre, in its `.meta`:

```text
[asset version=1 id=5f2c0a3e9d2b47c88e51f0d4a7b3c912]

texture.compression = Uncompressed
sprite.pixels_per_unit = 32
sprite.pivot = [0.5, 0]
sprite.filter = Linear
```

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `sprite.pixels_per_unit` | 16 | a 16-pixel tile is a metre; use 100 or so for high-resolution art |
| `sprite.pivot` | `[0.5, 0.5]` (the centre) | the point the sprite stands on, as shares of its width and height from its bottom-left corner; `[0.5, 0]` is its feet, what sorting by y wants |
| `sprite.filter` | `Nearest` | `Nearest` keeps pixel art sharp; `Linear` for painted art drawn at other sizes |
| `texture.compression` | `Auto` | `Uncompressed` keeps every pixel as painted (the editor's cache compresses with BC7 otherwise, which smudges pixel art) |

A sprite is its image's pixels over its pixels a metre unless its `size` says otherwise.

**Sheets and slices.** A sheet is cut into named rectangles, its slices, listed in the same
section (the editor's image tab will make them; by hand they read):

```text
sprite.slices.0.name = "run_0"
sprite.slices.0.rect = [0, 0, 16, 32]
sprite.slices.0.pivot = [0.5, 0]
sprite.slices.1.name = "run_1"
sprite.slices.1.rect = [16, 0, 16, 32]
```

In the editor, pick the image in the FileSystem dock and run **Image: Slice by Grid…**
(the cell's size: 8 to 128 pixels; cells with nothing drawn are left out) or **Image:
Slice by Islands** (a slice around each drawing, in reading order). Slicing again keeps the
names, pivots and borders of the slices that stay (the same rectangle, or one covering
more than half of the old), so scenes naming them do not break; new ones are named after
the file (`hero_0`, `hero_1`, …). Each is one undo step.

`rect` is left, top (from the image's top left), width and height in pixels; `pivot` is the
slice's own, by the image pivot's rule. A sprite of the sheet shows the slice its `frame`
numbers (0 the first; a number past the last shows the last), or one slice by name as a
part of the image: `asset("art/hero.png#run_1", <id>)` in a scene, `AssetRef::part(id,
"run_1")` in Rust. An image without slices is one frame, all of it. Picking in the editor
follows the slice too.

**Flipbooks.** A sheet's clips are runs of its frames, in the same section:

```text
sprite.clips.0.name = "run"
sprite.clips.0.first = 4
sprite.clips.0.last = 9
sprite.clips.0.frame_time = 0.08
sprite.clips.0.mode = PingPong
```

`first` and `last` are frame numbers (a last below the first runs backwards);
`frame_time` is seconds a frame (0.1 by default), `durations` gives frames their own;
`mode` is `Loop` (the default), `Once` (holds the last frame) or `PingPong`; `events` are
a model clip's events (seconds into the clip; scripts hear them in
`on_animation_event`). An `Animator` on the sprite's entity plays them by name, as it
plays a model's clips: `Animator::new("run")`, `animator:play("jump")` from a script; its
`speed`, `paused` and `time` work as for 3D characters, and saves keep where it was. Each
tick sets the sprite's `frame`. A clip of the animator's own `.anim` files wins over a
sheet's of the same name.

## 3. The orthographic camera

| Constructor | Result |
|---|---|
| `Camera::orthographic(height)` | shows `height` metres from the bottom of the window to its top, at the window's size and aspect |
| `Camera::pixel_art([w, h], pixels_per_unit)` | renders `w` × `h` pixels of art (each one a pixel at `pixels_per_unit` a metre) and shows them at the largest whole multiple that fits the window, centred, black around |

- Pixel art stays pixel-perfect: 320 × 180 fills 1280 × 720, 1920 × 1080 and 2560 × 1440
  exactly; 640 × 360 fills 1920 × 1080 three times over.
- Move and turn the camera with its `Transform` (turn it about z only). It looks down −Z.
- **Colours as painted:** a 2D view has no tone curve, no bloom and no grading, and an
  exposure of 1, so a pixel of a sprite reaches the screen with the value it was painted
  with (the engine's tests check every 8-bit value). Set `post_effects = 1` on the camera
  for glows: the view then goes through the player's own bloom, tone curve and grading
  (`Player::view_settings_mut`). Colours above 1 in a sprite's `color` glow with bloom.
- A 2D view has no temporal antialiasing or frame generation: sprites keep their hard
  edges.

## 4. Sprites

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `image` | the image, or one of its slices by name (`hero.png#idle_0`) |
| `frame` | which of the image's slices it shows (0 the first); an image without slices has one |
| `color` | multiplies the image: linear, straight alpha; above 1 glows with bloom |
| `flip_x`, `flip_y` | nonzero mirrors it |
| `size` | its size in metres; a 0 takes the image's |
| `mode` | how it fills its size: stretched (`Sprite::SIMPLE`); sliced and tiled come with slices |
| `layer`, `order` | where it is drawn (section 5) |
| `hidden` | nonzero hides it |

`Sprite::new(image).on_layer(layer, order).with_color(rgba).with_size(size)` builds one.
On-off fields are numbers (0 off, 1 on), so a sprite stays plain data a game can replicate
(`NetSchema::replicate::<Sprite>()`). Scripts see its fields as any component's.

## 5. What is drawn over what

1. **Layers:** a lower `layer` is drawn first (under).
2. **Within a layer:** by `order` (lower first), or by y on a layer that sorts by y: the
   higher a sprite stands (its pivot's y), the earlier it is drawn, so what is nearer the
   bottom of the screen is in front, as in top-down games. Sprites at the same y go by
   their order.
3. **Then** in the order they were recorded (the same every run).

The project names its layers and says which sort by y in `main.sorting` (its path is
`PlayerSettings::sorting`):

```text
[sorting version=1 id=0b6c1f0a3e9d2b47c88e51f0d4a7b3c9]

layers[-10].name = "background"
layers[-10].y_sort = false
layers[0].name = "characters"
layers[0].y_sort = true
```

**Sort groups.** A character of several sprites (its shadow, body and sword) must sort as
one: put a `SortGroup { layer, order }` on the entity above them. Its sprites are drawn
together at the group's place (its layer, and its own entity's y on a layer that sorts by
y), in the order of their own `order`: another character standing in front never cuts
between its body and its sword.

## 6. Motion

The simulation runs in fixed ticks (64 a second by default) and the frames between them
draw each sprite where it was between its last two ticks, as 3D objects are drawn: at 144
frames a second a sprite moving a metre a tick still moves a little every frame. Move
sprites in your game's tick (or with physics, scripts, animators); a sprite moved outside
the ticks is placed by the next one.

## 7. Performance

Measured on the reference machine (`engine_app` examples/sprite_bench.rs: 16-pixel sprites
moving every tick, sorted by y; ADR 0125):

| Sprites | 2D pass at 1080p, RTX 4060 Ti / Radeon 2-CU | at 640 × 360 | recording them (CPU) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 0.02 / 0.34 ms | 0.01 / 0.10 ms | 0.02 ms |
| 10,000 | 0.09 / 0.97 ms | 0.07 / 0.72 ms | 0.2 ms |
| 100,000 | 0.73 / 7.4 ms | 0.62 / 7.1 ms | 2.0–2.6 ms |

- A pixel-art resolution is the cheapest way to draw many sprites on a small GPU, and its
  scaling to the window is one full-screen pass either way.
- Sprites outside the camera's view are not recorded or drawn.
- Recording costs about 20 ns a sprite; a frame records nothing it does not draw, and
  nothing allocates.

## 8. Drawing by hand: a canvas

`engine_render2d` draws sprites and vector shapes. You describe a frame as a list of quads
in a `Canvas`; the renderer sorts them into your draw order, groups them into as few draws
as possible and renders them. Any number of textures can be used together: textures never
cost extra draw calls.

```rust
use engine_core::math::{Vec2, Vec4};
use engine_render2d::{Blend2d, Camera2d, Canvas, Order, Quad, View2d};

// Once: keep the canvas and reuse it every frame (it keeps its memory, so steady-state
// frames do not allocate).
let mut canvas = Canvas::new();

// Every frame:
canvas.clear();
canvas.draw(&Quad::sprite(hero_texture, hero_position, Vec2::new(1.0, 2.0))
    .order(Order::y_sorted(0, hero_position.y - 1.0)));
canvas.draw(&Quad::circle(Vec2::new(3.0, 1.0), 0.5).color(Vec4::new(1.0, 0.2, 0.2, 1.0)));
canvas.draw(&Quad::line(a, b, 0.1).blend(Blend2d::Additive));

// Sort and batch (on any thread, for example the job that recorded the canvas).
canvas.prepare();

let camera = Camera2d { center: player, pixels_per_unit: 32.0, rotation: 0.0 };
renderer.add_pass(&mut fg, &mut ring, &bindless, &canvas,
                  &camera.view(width, height), target, Some([0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0]))?;
```

`hero_texture` is a texture's bindless index: `GpuTexture::sampled_index()` in
`engine_render::texture`.

## 9. Quads

Every quad is a sprite or a shape.

| Constructor | Result |
|---|---|
| `Quad::sprite(texture, center, size)` | a textured rectangle with hard, pixel-exact edges |
| `Quad::solid(center, size)` | a solid rectangle with hard, pixel-exact edges |
| `Quad::rect(center, size)` | a solid rectangle with smooth (antialiased) edges |
| `Quad::rounded_rect(center, size, radius)` | rounded corners |
| `Quad::circle(center, radius)` | a disc |
| `Quad::line(a, b, width)` | a line with round caps |

Then adjust:
- `.color(rgba)`: linear color, straight alpha. It tints textures. Values above 1 are
  allowed (for HDR targets and glows).
- `.uv([u0, v0, u1, v1])`: a region of the texture, for sprite sheets. `v0` is the top.
- `.sampler(SamplerKind::NearestClamp)`: nearest filtering for pixel art.
- `.rotated(radians)`: counter-clockwise about the quad's center.
- `.outline(width)`: only the outline, drawn inside the edge.
- `.blend(Blend2d::Additive | Blend2d::Multiply)`: light or darken. The default is
  ordinary transparency.
- `.order(...)`: see below.

Per-corner radii: set `quad.shape = Some(Shape { radii: [tr, tl, bl, br], thickness })`.
The order is top-right, top-left, bottom-left, bottom-right, in a y-up view.

## 10. Draw order

Quads are drawn:
1. by **layer** (`Order::layer(n)`, lower first);
2. then by **key** within the layer (lower first);
3. then in the order you added them.

The result is the same every run.

- For top-down games, `Order::y_sorted(layer, y)` draws things further up first. Pass the
  y of the object's feet.
- Additive, alpha and multiply quads can be mixed freely; the order is always kept.

## 11. Clipping

`canvas.set_clip(Some(ClipRect { x, y, width, height }))` clips the quads that follow to a
rectangle in target pixels. `set_clip(None)` ends clipping. Use it for scroll views and
panels.

## 12. World and pixel space

- `Camera2d { center, pixels_per_unit, rotation }.view(width, height)` is for game worlds:
  y points up, and `pixels_per_unit` is the zoom.
- `View2d::pixels(width, height)` is for UI and overlays: origin at the top-left corner,
  y down, one unit per pixel.

Draw the world and the UI as two canvases, the second one with `clear = None` so it goes on
top.

## 13. Performance

- **Texture changes are free.** What starts a new draw call is a change between multiply
  and the other blend modes, or a change of clip rectangle. `canvas.stats()` shows the
  quad and batch count.
- **Keep canvases alive** across frames and call `clear()`: recording then does not
  allocate.
- **Sorting has a cost:**
  - a canvas already in order (one layer, no y-sorting, like most UI) is not sorted;
  - y-sorting 100 000 sprites costs about 1.4 ms on a 2.8 GHz server core (see ADR 0015).
- **Budget** at most about 64 + 4 bytes of upload-ring space per quad per frame
  (`UploadRing::new(..., capacity)`). `add_pass` returns `Render2dError::RingFull` instead of drawing
  when the ring is too small. Call `ring.grow(&device, &mut allocator)` once a frame (the 3D frame
  renderer does it for you): the ring then grows ahead when frames come close to its capacity, and
  `ring.stats()` shows the most a frame used.
